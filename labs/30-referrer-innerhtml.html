Lab 30: "You came from..."

Source: document.referrerSink: innerHTML

No referrer, or it wasn't carrying a search term.

Start from the simulated search results page and click through from there, rather than loading this page directly.

What is happening

This page shows a "you came from a search for ___" banner, a real pattern some sites use for analytics or personalization. It builds that banner from a q parameter it finds inside document.referrer: the URL of whatever page linked here.

Why document.referrer needs decoding first, and that's the trap

Browsers percent-encode document.referrer, so a raw innerHTML = document.referrer would just insert harmless, encoded text; you'd see %3Cimg...%3E on the page, not a real tag. The bug shows up one step later: URLSearchParams (used here to pull q out of the referrer URL) decodes its values automatically. That decode step is what turns the inert, encoded referrer back into a live payload.

Payload

Already linked above: a page at ?q=<img src=x onerror=alert(1)> that links to this one. Click through from there rather than editing this page's own URL, since this bug reads the previous page's address, not this one's.

Fix 
banner.textContent = "You came from a search for: " + q;
